How to turn a creative brief into three distinct visual directions with AI

By Yam Ben Adiva

  • Use cases
  • Art direction
  • Workflow
  • Image

To develop distinct visual directions with AI, translate the brief into clear criteria, define a different idea for each route, and build a small set of images that makes each idea understandable. In Plnty, the shared canvas lets you keep the brief, references and experiments together while moving between image-gen, style-transfer, explorer and variations.

The goal is a set of directions that someone can meaningfully choose between.

01 / Criteria

Turn the brief into criteria you can see

Guide fact sheet

Tools: image-gen, style-transfer, explorer, variations

You start with: a launch brief for a citrus sparkling drink

You end with: three distinct directions, each with a lead image

Begin with the message the work needs to communicate, the audience it needs to reach, and the qualities it must retain.

Consider this illustrative brief:

Create launch visuals for a citrus sparkling drink. The audience is design-conscious adults. The work should feel bright, playful and refreshing. The product must remain recognizable, and the main image needs room for a short headline.

Before generating, translate those words into visual questions.

What would make “refreshing” visible: condensation, open space, a cool surface, or a sense of movement? Could “playful” come from an unexpected scale relationship rather than a smiling character? What makes the packaging recognizable?

Keep the brief short enough to consult while working. Separate fixed requirements from things you are free to explore.

For this example, the product reference and core message stay consistent. Materials, setting, composition and visual metaphor can change.

02 / Ideas

Define three ideas before making three looks

Give each route a one-sentence concept.

For the drink brief, three possible directions are:

These routes differ in how they communicate the message. Changing the background color alone would not create that distinction.

A direction name is useful when it helps you decide what belongs. If an image looks good but does not support the route’s central idea, move it aside.

Build a starting image for each route

In Plnty

image-gen, text-to-image mode, to invent a scene

style-transfer, the product photograph as Subject, when the real product guides the image

Use image-gen in text-to-image mode when you need to invent a scene from a written description. Describe the subject, arrangement, materials and mood that matter to that direction.

For the cut-paper route, a starting instruction could be:

An editorial still life built from cut-paper citrus shapes, with a clear central area for a drink product. Bold graphic forms, visible paper edges, directional shadows and open space for a short headline.

Treat this as a starting scene, not proof that the exact product or packaging will appear correctly.

When the real product needs to guide the image, bring its photograph into a reference-based workflow. style-transfer lets you distinguish the product as Subject from an image that supplies Style.

Start with one clear style reference per direction. Add more only when each contributes something specific. An overcrowded reference set can make it difficult to tell which qualities are driving the result.

Arrange the three routes in separate areas of the canvas, with their brief sentences and references nearby.

03 / Explorer

Use explorer when a route feels too predictable

In Plnty

explorer, Tangents mode

No written prompt: a lower Imagination setting for smaller departures, a higher one to move further.

Sometimes the first image communicates the brief but offers little surprise.

explorer, in its Tangents mode, explores departures from an existing image through an Imagination control. It does not take a written prompt. Lower settings are intended for smaller departures; higher settings allow the visual treatment to move further.

Use it when you want to discover something you would not have specified in advance.

For the surreal citrus route, that might mean exploring an image already built around an unusual scale relationship, then looking for an unexpected arrangement worth developing.

Judge the results against the route’s idea. An unusual result is useful when it strengthens the concept. If it changes the subject’s meaning or makes the product unrecognizable, it may belong in a different experiment.

Keep interesting discoveries visible, including ones you decide not to pursue. They can clarify the boundary of the direction.

Use variations to develop a promising idea

In Plnty

variations, Composition, Colors or Proportions mode

One dimension at a time, within a route you have already chosen.

Once a route has a clear identity, use variations to explore a particular dimension within it.

For the summer still life, investigate Composition to find stronger framing. For the paper direction, investigate Colors. For the surreal route, Proportions may help you explore the scale relationship.

This is a different task from inventing a new route. You are looking for a stronger expression of an idea you have already chosen.

Avoid changing every dimension at once. Keeping the central idea stable makes it easier to judge whether a new color treatment, crop or proportion improves the work.

Build a small selection for each route: a lead image and a few supporting studies. You need enough to explain the direction, not every generation made along the way.

04 / Compare

Compare the directions against the same brief

Review all three routes together.

Ask the same questions of each:

  • Does the main message come through?
  • Is the product recognizable?
  • Does the direction feel appropriate for the audience?
  • Is there room for the intended copy?
  • Could the idea support more than one image?
  • What still requires correction or production work?

Name the trade-offs. The still-life route might be easier to read immediately. The paper route might offer a stronger graphic language. The surreal route might be more distinctive but require closer attention to product detail.

Those are hypotheses to assess in the actual work, not conclusions to assume before making it.

Refine the chosen route into a usable next step

In Plnty

image-gen, edit-image mode

Specific revisions, such as simplifying a background or removing a distracting element.

Once a direction is selected, write a short explanation of what makes it work: its composition, material treatment, palette and central idea.

Then use image-gen in edit-image mode for specific revisions, such as simplifying a background or removing a distracting element. Keep checking details that generation may change, particularly packaging, lettering and product shape.

The useful deliverable is an image accompanied by a clear creative decision. A teammate should be able to understand what to preserve when developing the next asset.
